Investor Education & Awareness

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund is a pool of money managed by a professional fund manager. It trustfully invests the collected money in securities like stocks, bonds, and other short-term debt instruments. The combined holdings of the mutual fund are known as its portfolio. Each unit represents an investor's proportionate ownership of the portfolio's holdings and the income those holdings generate.

Direct Plan vs. Regular Plan

Mutual funds offer two plans: Direct and Regular. In a Direct Plan, an investor buys units directly from the Mutual Fund Company (AMC) without any intermediary, resulting in a lower expense ratio because no distributor commission is paid. In a Regular Plan, units are bought through a broker, distributor, or agent, leading to a higher expense ratio because of agent commissions.

Understanding Mutual Fund Riskometers

SEBI mandates a riskometer to depict the risk level of mutual fund schemes. The six levels of risk are: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, and Very High. Investors should review the riskometer to align the scheme's risk level with their personal risk tolerance.
