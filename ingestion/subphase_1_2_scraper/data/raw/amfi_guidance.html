Understanding Mutual Fund Categories

What are Large Cap Funds?

Large Cap Funds invest a minimum of 80% of their total assets in equity and equity-related instruments of large-cap companies. Large-cap companies are defined as the top 100 companies by full market capitalization as per AMFI classification. These funds are generally less volatile than mid-cap or small-cap funds and offer relatively stable returns. Examples include HDFC Large Cap Fund, which invests predominantly in Nifty 100 stocks.

What are Mid Cap Funds?

Mid Cap Funds invest a minimum of 65% of their total assets in equity instruments of mid-cap companies. Mid-cap companies are ranked 101st to 250th by full market capitalization. These funds offer higher growth potential compared to large-cap funds but come with higher volatility. The benchmark for mid-cap funds is typically the Nifty Midcap 150 TRI.

What are Flexi Cap Funds?

Flexi Cap Funds invest a minimum of 65% of their total assets in equity instruments across market capitalizations (large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap). The fund manager has the flexibility to dynamically allocate between different market segments based on valuations and opportunities. This category was introduced by SEBI in November 2020.

What are Focused Funds?

Focused Funds invest in a maximum of 30 stocks across market capitalizations. These funds maintain a concentrated portfolio, which means they can have higher upside potential but also higher stock-specific risk compared to diversified funds. A minimum of 65% must be invested in equity instruments.

What are ELSS Funds?

ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) funds are tax-saving mutual funds that qualify for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Key features: mandatory lock-in period of 3 years (shortest among 80C options), minimum 80% allocation to equity, investment limit of Rs. 1,50,000 per financial year for tax deduction, and potential for higher returns compared to other 80C instruments like PPF, NSC, and tax-saving FDs.

Key Mutual Fund Concepts

What is Expense Ratio (TER)?

The Total Expense Ratio (TER) is the annual fee charged by the AMC for managing the fund. It includes management fees, administrative costs, registrar fees, custodian charges, audit fees, and marketing/distribution expenses. SEBI has set maximum TER limits based on fund category and AUM. Direct plans have lower TER than regular plans. For equity funds, the maximum TER for the first Rs. 500 crore AUM is 2.25% (regular plan). As AUM increases, the permitted TER decreases.

What is Exit Load?

Exit load is a fee charged when an investor redeems units before a specified period. It is typically 1% for equity funds if redeemed within 1 year. The exit load is deducted from the redemption NAV. ELSS funds have no exit load because they have a mandatory 3-year lock-in period. Liquid funds may have graded exit load for redemptions within 7 days.

What is NAV?

Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the per-unit price of a mutual fund scheme. Formula: NAV = (Total Value of Portfolio - Liabilities) / Total Number of Outstanding Units. NAV is declared at the end of every business day after market hours. A higher NAV does not mean the fund is expensive — what matters is the percentage return, not the absolute NAV.

What is Benchmark?

A benchmark is a standard index against which the performance of a mutual fund is measured. SEBI requires each scheme to declare a benchmark index. Common benchmarks include Nifty 50 TRI, Nifty 100 TRI, Nifty 500 TRI, Nifty Midcap 150 TRI, and Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI. A fund that consistently outperforms its benchmark is said to generate alpha. The benchmark helps investors evaluate whether the fund manager is adding value compared to passive investing.

Investment Best Practices

How to Choose a Mutual Fund?

Consider these factors when selecting a mutual fund: investment objective and time horizon, risk tolerance, fund's past performance relative to benchmark (over 3, 5, and 10 years), expense ratio (lower is better), fund manager experience and track record, AUM size (very small or very large AUM may pose challenges), consistency of returns across market cycles, and portfolio quality.

What is Rupee Cost Averaging?

Rupee cost averaging is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of market conditions. When the market is down, you buy more units at a lower NAV. When the market is up, you buy fewer units at a higher NAV. Over time, this averages out the cost per unit and reduces the impact of market volatility. SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is the most common way to implement rupee cost averaging in mutual funds.

What is Asset Allocation?

Asset allocation is the strategy of dividing investments across different asset classes (equity, debt, gold, real estate) based on the investor's risk tolerance, time horizon, and financial goals. A common rule of thumb is to subtract your age from 100 to determine the equity allocation percentage. For example, a 30-year-old might allocate 70% to equity and 30% to debt. Asset allocation should be reviewed periodically and rebalanced based on changing market conditions and life stages.

Investor Protection & SEBI Regulations

How does SEBI protect mutual fund investors?

SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) regulates the mutual fund industry through various measures: mandatory disclosure of NAV, portfolio, and expenses; standardized scheme categorization to prevent misleading names; maximum TER limits based on AUM; mandatory use of benchmarks for performance measurement; requirement for skin-in-the-game (fund managers investing in their own schemes); prohibition of entry load; and investor grievance redressal mechanisms through SCORES portal.

What is KYC and why is it mandatory?

KYC (Know Your Customer) is a one-time verification process mandated by SEBI and PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act) for all mutual fund investors. Required documents include valid photo ID (Aadhaar, PAN card, passport), address proof, and a recent photograph. KYC can be completed at any KRA (KYC Registration Agency) such as CAMS KRA, KFintech KRA, or CVL KRA. Once completed, KYC is valid across all mutual fund houses.

What is AMFI?

AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds in India) is the industry body that represents all mutual fund houses in India. AMFI's key responsibilities include: maintaining the mutual fund distributor certification exam (AMFI Registration Number or ARN), publishing the AMFI NAV list daily, maintaining stock classification for large-cap/mid-cap/small-cap categories (updated semi-annually), investor awareness campaigns ("Mutual Funds Sahi Hai"), and setting ethical standards for the mutual fund industry.
